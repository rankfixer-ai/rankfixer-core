## Executive Summary

We measured the AI Visibility of 100 well-known SaaS websites with the RankFixer scoring engine — how likely they are to be cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews.

Of the 100 domains, **90 were fully analyzed**. **9 blocked our crawler (HTTP 403)** and **1 was unreachable**; those 10 are excluded from the statistics below and listed at the end of this report.

**Key findings (90 analyzed domains):**

1. **Average score 62.7/100; median 63.**
2. **6 domains (6.7%) score "Excellent" (81–100)**; 45 (50%) score "Good" (61–80).
3. **No analyzed domain scored "Critical" (0–20).** The weakest scored "Poor" — the old narrative of "one-third of SaaS is invisible to AI" does not hold on real data.
4. **72% of domains publish an llms.txt file** — adoption has gone mainstream among top SaaS.
5. **Schema markup is the biggest gap** — average schema score is 26.6/100.

## Signal-by-Signal Analysis

### 1. Schema Markup (25% weight, average 26.6/100) — the biggest gap

The scorer counts JSON-LD blocks and `@type` markers (`Organization`, `FAQPage`, `WebSite`, `Product`, `Article`, `HowTo`). This is the lowest-scoring signal across the board: most domains ship little or no structured schema on their homepage.

**Quickest win:** add a JSON-LD `Organization` + `WebSite` block (and `FAQPage` where relevant) to the homepage.

### 2. Entity Signals (20%, average 61.3/100)

Measures `@id` references, `sameAs` links, structured brand markup, and Open Graph / Twitter Card tags. Most domains pass on OG/Twitter tags, but far fewer use `@id`/`sameAs` entity linking.

### 3. Content Depth (20%, average 80.3/100)

Word count, FAQ-style headings, lists/tables, and meta description presence. Marketing-led homepages score well here.

### 4. Structure (15%, average 82.5/100)

Heading hierarchy (`h1`/`h2`), `nav`/`main` landmarks, and microdata markers. Strong across the board.

### 5. Crawlability (10%, average 80/100)

Homepage HTTP 200 plus `robots.txt` presence. Note: the 9 domains that returned 403 are excluded from scoring rather than given a misleading low score.

### 6. llms.txt (10%, average 72.2/100)

65 of 90 domains publish an `llms.txt` file. This is a notable shift — a discovery mechanism that was nearly nonexistent two years ago is now mainstream among top SaaS.

## Recommendations

1. **Add JSON-LD `Organization` + `WebSite` + `FAQPage` schema** — the single largest, cheapest gap.
2. **Add `@id`/`sameAs` entity linking** to connect your brand to authoritative sources.
3. **Publish an llms.txt** — 28% of analyzed domains still lack one.
4. **Keep AI crawlers reachable** — 10 domains in this set block the RankFixerBot entirely.

## Methodology

- **Engine:** RankFixer scoring engine (`site/netlify/functions/score.js`)
- **Date:** August 2026
- **Sample:** 100 curated SaaS domains (`data/domains_to_analyze.txt`)
- **Signals (weighted):** Schema 25%, Entity 20%, Content 20%, Structure 15%, Crawlability 10%, llms.txt 10%
- **Method:** fetch each domain's homepage, `robots.txt`, and `llms.txt` (RankFixerBot user agent); parse HTML for schema/entity/content/structure; score 0–100.
- **Exclusions:** blocked (HTTP 401/403/406/418/429) and unreachable domains are marked and excluded from averages.
- **Limitations:** English-language sites; single-pass snapshot; a few domains show intermittent anti-bot behavior (e.g., dropbox.com scored 29 in this pass vs. 71 in a prior pass).
