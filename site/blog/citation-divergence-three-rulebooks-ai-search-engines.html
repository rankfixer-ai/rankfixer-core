Citation Divergence: The Three Rulebooks

September 16, 2026 ai-search, citations, measurement, analytics
A 320-query benchmark reveals that the four major AI engines do not share a single citation standard. They operate under three fundamentally different rulebooks, and optimizing for one will not optimize you for the others.

The myth of "AI SEO"

For the past two years, a single assumption has dominated search strategy: if you rank well in Google, AI engines will cite you too. The logic seemed sound. AI answers pull from the web. The web is indexed by Google. Rank well, get cited.

That logic is increasingly hard to defend against the data.

In July 2026, researchers at CiteLens published a benchmark that tested 320 real buyer queries across four AI engines — Google AI Mode, Perplexity, Claude, and ChatGPT — then compared every cited source against the same query's organic results in Google and Bing. The finding: the four engines sorted into three distinct citation regimes, and two of them barely resemble traditional search at all. [1][2]

Scope and provenance: The CiteLens benchmark covered 320 queries. Two of the five cited sources describe its scope differently: MarTech Series reports three consumer sectors in the Turkish market; Obsurfable states "no cherry-picked verticals, no single-industry sample." This post treats the 93%/89%/53%/30% figures as directional signals pending replication on a broader sample.

This changes how every team working on AI visibility should allocate effort, track success, and define what "cited" actually means.

The three regimes, by the numbers

Regime 1: Google-aligned engines (Google AI Mode and Perplexity)

Google AI Mode pulls 93% of its citations from Google's top-10 organic results. Perplexity pulls 89%. The correlation between Google ranking and citation frequency is 0.92 for AI Mode and 0.87 for Perplexity — near-lockstep alignment. [1]

For these two engines, classic SEO fundamentals remain the primary path to citation. If a page ranks in the top 10, it will very likely be cited when these engines answer relevant queries. Structured content, topical depth, backlinks, and clear formatting still move the needle directly. [3]

But the two engines are not identical within the regime. Perplexity shows a heavy preference for Reddit, which accounts for 46.7% of its top citation share. Freshness also matters: 82% of Perplexity's citations come from pages under 30 days old. Google AI Mode, meanwhile, increasingly cites Google-hosted surfaces like Business Profiles and Product Knowledge Panels. [2]

The practical takeaway: ranking works, but source-type strategy must still be engine-specific.

Regime 2: The hybrid engine (Claude)

Claude splits the difference. It pulls 53% of its citations from Google's top-10, meaning the remaining citations come from somewhere else entirely. [1] Claude retrieves through Brave Search rather than Google directly, and it also uses its own supplemental crawler, Claude-SearchBot. [2]

What fills the non-top-10 half? Wikipedia-backed sources predicted 58% of Claude's citations — the strongest single predictor. Claude favors reference-grade documentation, precise data points with named sources, and content that reads as a primary source. [1][3]

This means Claude is partially responsive to traditional SEO work but also rewards content that exists in authoritative training data, academic papers, technical documentation, and structured knowledge sources. [3]

Regime 3: The independent engine (ChatGPT)

ChatGPT is the outlier. Only 30% of its citations come from Google's top-10 organic results. The other 70% rank in neither Google's nor Bing's top-10. [1][3] Its correlation with Google ranking is near zero. Its correlation with brand popularity is near zero. [1]

The convenient assumption that ChatGPT simply mirrors Bing? It does not hold. Fewer than 4% of ChatGPT's citations appear in Bing's top-10. [1] Only 21% of its citations go to Wikipedia-backed sites — versus Claude's 58%. [1] ChatGPT surfaces niche domains that rank nowhere and command little search demand. [1]

What actually drives ChatGPT citations is entity authority: a brand's consistent, recognized presence across independent publishers, forums, and reference sources, regardless of where any single page sits in Google or Bing. [3]

The overlap problem

The divergence between engines is not theoretical. It shows up in cross-engine domain overlap data from multiple studies published throughout 2026.

CiteMetrix, analyzing 680 million citations, found that only 11% of domains cited by ChatGPT are also cited by Perplexity. [2] The same query, nearly opposite source sets. Provenance note: The 680M-citation dataset is attributed to CiteMetrix by Obsurfable, but Averi.ai claims the same figure as its own proprietary analysis. The original source is unresolved — treat this stat as directional.

BrightEdge AI Catalyst found that pairwise top-100 source overlap across five AI engines ranges from 16% to 59% — a 43-point spread. [4] The engines disagree substantially and inconsistently about where to pull information from. By contrast, brand overlap across the same engines clusters in a tighter 36% to 55% range — a 19-point spread. [4] The engines agree more consistently about which brands to recommend than about which sources to consult.

Foglift measured ChatGPT-Claude citation Jaccard overlap at 0.027 — effectively near-zero. [2]

The pattern is unambiguous: AI search is not converging on a single citation standard. It is diverging. [3]

What actually drives citations (across all engines)

While the engines disagree on which sources to pull from, they agree on one thing: paid content does not work. Muck Rack's May 2026 analysis of more than 25 million links found that paid and advertorial content accounts for just 0.3% of AI citations across ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. [5] You cannot buy your way into the citation set.

What does work? Earned media. Muck Rack found that earned media — journalism, academic research, government sources, encyclopedic sites, and third-party corporate content — accounts for 82–89% of all AI citations across three study editions. Journalism alone makes up 25–27%. [5]

Those numbers have held steady across three editions of the study going back to July 2025, suggesting these are structural patterns, not model-update quirks. [5]

ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini also differ sharply in how often they cite. ChatGPT cites sources in 96% of responses but averages only five citations per response. Gemini cites in 82% of responses, averaging eight. Claude is the most selective, citing in 55% of responses but averaging 13 sources when it does. [5]

What to stop doing

Stop running one content playbook across all engines. The data is clear that this underperforms on at least two of the three regimes. [3]

A YouTube page that drives 11.2% of Google AI Mode citations drives 0.02% of Claude's. [2] A Reddit thread that dominates Perplexity will not appear in ChatGPT. A page that ranks #20 in Google but has strong entity signals, clear schema, and brand authority can still get cited by ChatGPT while a #2-ranked page with weaker entity signals gets skipped. [3]

Stop tracking citation share as a single blended number. A brand can rank well and be cited constantly on Perplexity while remaining nearly invisible on ChatGPT. The difference only shows up when each engine is measured on its own. [3]

Stop assuming AI Mode and AI Overviews are interchangeable. Obsurfable measures just 13.7% overlap in the exact URLs they cite; BrightEdge measures 59% overlap in source domains. Same conclusion, different measurement bases — treat them as distinct systems. [2][4]

What to do instead

For Google AI Mode and Perplexity: Keep investing in traditional SEO. Top-10 rankings are the most reliable path. FAQ schema, clear heading structure, and topical coverage still convert almost directly to citations. [3] For Perplexity specifically, add Reddit placements and a 60-to-90 day content refresh cycle to the mix. [2]

For Claude: Claude retrieves through Brave Search and uses its own supplemental crawler, Claude-SearchBot. Confirming Brave index presence and allowing Claude-SearchBot in robots.txt is a reasonable first check. Wikipedia-backed sources correlate with 58% of Claude citations — worth testing whether pursuing this moves the metric; the underlying data is correlational, not causal. [2][3]

For ChatGPT: Build entity authority deliberately. Independent brand mentions across publishers, inclusion in comparison and "best of" pieces you do not control, and a Wikipedia entry where the business qualifies. [3] Earned media is what this engine trusts — 84% of citations come from sources outside the cited brand's own domain. [5]

For every engine: Track citation share separately per engine on the same prompt set. Build four dashboards, not one aggregate. [2] Report engine-by-engine. Aggregate scores hide the gaps that matter. [2]

The earned authority shift

The CiteLens data points to a structural shift in how authority is earned online. For two of the four engines, page-level ranking is still the currency. For the other two, entity-level authority — whether the model already recognizes your brand as a named, credible entity — is what wins the citation. [1]

This is not a temporary gap. As of July 2026 — the date of the Mintec study — the engines' citation logic is diverging, not converging. [3] Treating them as separate systems is the safer near-term approach. [3]

RankFixer's thesis has always been that there is no single AI SEO. The CiteLens benchmark now gives that thesis hard numbers: 93%, 89%, 53%, 30%. Four engines, three rulebooks, and a 11% overlap between the two most popular ones.

The question for your brand is not "are you cited by AI." It is "are you cited by the engine whose behavior you can actually optimize for?"

Methodology note

All five sources are published by commercial entities with content-marketing incentives. Their relationship to the data differs:

  • CiteLens — describes itself as an independent research group. Obsurfable explicitly states: "CiteLens is not a platform vendor study. It is an independent analysis." However, MarTech Series reports the benchmark covered three consumer sectors in the Turkish market, while Obsurfable denies any cherry-picked verticals. This contradiction about the same study's scope is unresolved in the published record. Treat the 93%/89%/53%/30% figures as directional, not generalizable.
  • Obsurfable, Muck Rack, BrightEdge — all three sell AI-visibility measurement tools; their studies are vendor-produced benchmark content marketing.
  • Mintec — a general web-development and marketing agency (Honduras/Colombia/US), not an AI-visibility vendor. Its content incentive is marketing-agency lead generation, weaker than the other four.

Some headline figures recur across independently-attributed secondary sources: "93%" appears in both Paralax and Mintec, each transparently citing the CiteLens primary; "84%" appears in both Obsurfable and Muck Rack, with Obsurfable directly attributing it to Muck Rack. This corroboration is expected when multiple outlets cite the same primary — it is not sourcing circularity.

The genuinely unresolved figure is the 11% ChatGPT-Perplexity domain overlap: CiteMetrix and Averi.ai both claim the 680M-citation dataset, with no traceable original. It is flagged inline above.

Sample sizes (320 queries, 25M links, 680M citations), time windows, and query-construction methods are not directly comparable. This post synthesizes directional signals, not controlled experiments.

Some recommendations in this post derive closely from Obsurfable's engine-specific playbooks; prescriptive language has been softened accordingly.
