The 6 Signals LLMs Use to Decide What to Cite

Published 2026-07-11 · 6 min read

When an LLM answers a question, it doesn't flip a coin on sources. It leans on structural signals that make a page easy to extract, verify, and rephrase. Rankfixer scores your site on six of them.

1. Schema completeness

Structured data (JSON-LD) tells a model exactly what your page is — an Article, a Product, an FAQ. Pages with correct schema are far more likely to be parsed as a citable fact.

2. Entity consistency

Clear, repeated naming of your brand, product, and topic entities — plus @id and sameAs links in your structured data — helps a model build a stable "mental model" of what you are, and cite you consistently.

3. Content structure

Models love a question followed immediately by a direct answer. Lead with the answer; elaborate after. Substantial text with FAQ-style questions, bulleted and numbered lists, and a meta description are extracted cleanly.

4. Technical structure

Semantic HTML — a single H1, a proper H2 hierarchy, nav and main landmarks — makes your page parseable without guesswork. Clean structure is cheap to fix and pays off across every crawler.

5. Crawlability for AI bots

If you block GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or PerplexityBot in robots.txt, you've opted out of being cited. Make sure AI crawlers are allowed.

6. llms.txt

A dedicated llms.txt file gives AI crawlers a curated map of your site — what it is, what matters, and where to look. In our scan of 90 top SaaS domains, 72% published one.
